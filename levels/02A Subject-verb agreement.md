**The rule**

Find the head of the subject, then match the verb to it. A phrase such as *from the court reporter* adds information, but it doesn’t change whether *notes* is singular or plural.

✓ The contract for the new office is ready.
✗ The contract for the new office are ready. → *is*

**Explanation**

A *phrase* is a group of words built around one main word, called its *head*. Other words or phrases that add information to the head are its *dependents*. In *the contract for the new office*, *contract* is the head, and *for the new office* is an *adjectival dependent*.

Try crossing out the phrase and reading the head with the verb.

| Ask | Head | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| What’s the head? | *contract* (one) | *is* |
| What’s the head? | *signatures* (more than one) | *are* |

The deadline ~~for the filings~~ is approaching.

**Tricky cases**

- *And* joins two subjects, so it takes a plural verb: The clerk and two couriers are waiting outside.
- *Along with* adds information but doesn’t join another subject: The clerk, along with two couriers, is waiting outside.
- When *a number of* means *several*, use a plural verb: A number of hearing dates have changed.
- When *all of* or *some of* refers to plural items, the verb is plural: All of the pages are signed.
