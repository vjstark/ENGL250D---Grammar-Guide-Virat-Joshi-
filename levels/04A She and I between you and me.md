**The rule**

- Use subject forms such as *I, she, he, we,* and *they* for the subject of a verb.
- Use object forms such as *me, her, him, us,* and *them* after a verb or preposition.
- Use reflexive forms such as *myself* when they refer back to the subject or add emphasis; don’t use them just to sound more formal.

✓ Laila and I reviewed the transmittal letter.
✗ Laila and me reviewed the transmittal letter. → *I*
✓ The docket clerk sent the confirmation to Laila and me.
✗ The docket clerk sent the confirmation to Laila and I. → *me*

**Explanation**

Subject pronouns are in the subjective case; they identify who or what the sentence is about. Object pronouns are in the objective case; they receive an action or follow a preposition.

In a phrase with more than one person, cover the other name and check the pronoun by itself: “The attorney thanked Celia and me” becomes “The attorney thanked me,” not “The attorney thanked I.”

**Tricky cases**

- Use a reflexive pronoun when the subject and object are the same person: “The assistant reminded herself to save the letter.” Don’t use *myself* instead of *me*: “Please send the final copy to me,” not “to myself.”
- In a formal comparison, use the subject form when the pronoun is the subject of an understood verb: “The mediator spoke longer than I did.”
- After *between*, use the object form: “between the auditor and me.”
