## Question
Which sentence is the most formal?

A) Who did you send the file to?
B) To whom did you send the file?
C) Whom did you send the file to?
Answer: B
Feedback A: This is correct, but casual: it uses who and leaves to at the end.
Feedback C: Whom is the formal pronoun, but the preposition is still at the end. B is more formal.
Hint: Which sentence keeps the preposition next to its object?

## Question
Which sentence ends with a preposition that should be cut?

A) Where is the hearing at?
B) Which room is the hearing in?
C) What is the hearing about?
Answer: A
Feedback B: Without in, the sentence no longer makes sense. The preposition is needed.
Feedback C: Without about, the meaning changes. The preposition is needed.
Hint: Remove the last word. Does the sentence still mean the same thing?

## Question
Is this sentence correct? She is the lawyer whom I spoke with.

A) Yes
B) No, because a sentence can't end with a preposition
C) No, because whom should be who
Answer: A
Feedback B: That rule is a myth. Ending with a preposition is correct English.
Feedback C: Inside the clause, I spoke with her, so the pronoun is an object and whom is correct.
Hint: Check the pronoun with the he/him swap from Level 5.
