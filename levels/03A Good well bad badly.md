**The rule**

- Use the adjectives *good* and *bad* after linking verbs to describe the subject.
- Use the adverbs *well* and *badly* to describe an action.

✓ The revised wording sounds good.
✗ The revised wording sounds well. → *good*
✓ The assistant explained the change well.
✗ The assistant explained the change good. → *well*

**Explanation**

Linking verbs are *stative* verbs: they express a state rather than an action. An adjective after a linking verb is a *predicative complement* because it describes or renames the subject. An adverb such as *well* can be a *modifier* of an action verb.

To use the *be test*, try replacing the verb with *is* or *was*. If the sentence still makes sense, use an adjective after the verb: “The new heading seems bad” still makes sense as “The new heading is bad.”

**Tricky cases**

- *Well* can also be an adjective meaning healthy. Both “Are you feeling well?” and “Are you feeling good?” are correct: *well* asks about health, while *good* asks about mood or general condition.
- Some verbs can be linking verbs or action verbs. In “The print looks bad,” *looks* connects the subject to a description. In “The editor looked closely at the print,” *looked* describes an action.
- In “I feel bad for your mom,” *feel* is a linking verb, so use *bad* for sympathy or regret.
