# Grammar Guide

## Introduction

We’re building a grammar learning game where players work as proofreaders at a law firm. Players start with Day 0, the tutorial, where they learn how to play. Then they work through seven levels, and each level is a workday that teaches a new grammar topic.

Players use what they learn each day to defeat the mini-boss: a memo that is due at 5 p.m. Each level builds on what players learned in the earlier levels while teaching them something new.

The levels are:

- Day 1: The Supply Order (Words)
- Day 2: The Exhibit List (Phrases)
- Day 3: The Client Update (Verbs)
- Day 4: Between You and Me (Roles)
- Day 5: The Witness (Clauses)
- Day 6: Where It’s At (Word Order)
- Day 7: A Matter of Commas (Punctuation)

In the final boss battle, players will proofread a court affidavit using what they learned in all seven levels.

In each level, we will look at examples we found difficult while learning in class or came across on our own. For example, Day 5 looks at when to use *who* and when to use *whom*.

## Level 1: Fewer vs. Less

***Day 1: The Supply Order (Words)***  
*Topic: fewer vs. less*

### The question
How do you know whether the office needs fewer binders or less paper? Both words describe a smaller quantity, so they’re easy to mix up.

### The rule
- Use *fewer* with things you can count one at a time.
- Use *less* with an amount you don’t count one by one (Cambridge University Press & Assessment, n.d.; Oxford University Press, n.d.).

### Example
- Correct Use: Please order fewer binders and less paper.
- **Incorrect Use: Please order less binders. → *fewer* binders**

### Explanation
Ask yourself, “How many?” or “How much?”

| Ask | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| How many? | *fewer* | fewer binders |
| How much? | *less* | less glue |

- *Binders* is a plural countable noun because you can count each binder.
- *Paper* is a non-countable noun here because it means the material, not separate documents.
- *Fewer* and *less* are determiners: they show a smaller number or amount.

### Tricky cases
- Use *less* with measurements of time, money, and distance:
  - *less than three hours*
  - *less than $50*
  - *less than ten kilometres*
- Use *fewer* for individual people and things: *fewer than ten clients*.
- If *paper* means separate documents, it can be counted: *fewer papers*.

### Unique approach
**The Supply Order Under Cross-Examination**

Ms. Fewster: “Can we count the binders one by one?”  
Clerk: “Yes.”  
Ms. Fewster: “Then use *fewer binders*. And the paper?”  
Clerk: “We measure it as an amount.”  
Ms. Fewster: “Then use *less paper*.”

### Skeptic’s corner
You may hear people say *less binders* in everyday conversation, and most people will understand them. For formal proofreading, use *fewer* with countable items and save *less* for amounts and measurements.

Grammarphobia traces this rule to 1770, when grammarian Robert Baker argued that *fewer* was more proper than *less* in a phrase like “no less than a hundred.” The article notes that writers had used *less* to mean *fewer* for centuries before then (O’Conner & Kellerman, 2010).

### Practice
1. The office needs ___ folders than it ordered last month.
   - (A) fewer
   - (B) less
   - **Answer: A.** You can count folders one by one.

2. The courier spent ___ than $50 delivering the boxes.
   - (A) fewer
   - (B) less
   - **Answer: B.** Fifty dollars is an amount of money.

3. The office used ___ paper than last month but filed ___ papers.
   - (A) less / fewer
   - (B) fewer / less
   - **Answer: A.** *Paper* means the material, so it’s non-countable. *Papers* means separate documents, so you can count them.

4. The clerk finished the review in ___ than 90 minutes and found ___ errors than the first reviewer.
   - (A) less / fewer
   - (B) fewer / less
   - **Answer: A.** Use *less* for a measurement of time and *fewer* for countable errors.

### Inspect passage
*Email from Omar Haddad, Senior Paralegal. Subject: Courier budget update. There are two errors.*

> We had less courier deliveries this month than last month, so the invoice was lower. The courier charged fewer than $200 for the deliveries. We also used less paper by sending receipts electronically. Please review the attached summary before I send it to Ms. Fewster.

**Answer key**
1. *less courier deliveries* → *fewer*: deliveries can be counted one by one.
2. *fewer than $200* → *less*: money is an amount.
- Trap: *less paper* is correct, because paper is the material here, an amount.
